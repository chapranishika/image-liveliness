### Finding: Confirmed Demographic Bias (not merely untested)
* `day36_38_bias_testing.py` was executed against 80 real CFP images across skin tone, gender, and age subgroups. This moved bias from an "unknown" caveat to a **measured, disclosed problem**:
  * Quality pass rate (Balanced profile): Female 20.0% (8/40) vs. Male 42.5% (17/40).
  * Passive liveness pass rate: Senior 25.0% (6/24) vs. Middle-aged 46.2% (12/26).
  * Skin tone gap was smaller but present (Dark 26.9% vs. Light 35.7% quality pass rate).
* **Sample sizes (24-40 per subgroup) are large enough to take seriously as a signal, not large enough to be regulatory-grade proof.**
* **Status: UNMITIGATED.** No rebalancing, threshold adjustment per subgroup, or retraining has been attempted. This should be disclosed explicitly to any client or reviewer alongside the calibration numbers, not left implicit in a script's console output. **ACTION**: at minimum, add this finding to any client-facing evaluation summary before demo/handoff; full mitigation is out of scope for the current timeline.
* **Update (2026-08-08, Phase 0 re-measurement, deferred to Phase 4 — numbers drifted, recorded here rather than silently kept stale):** re-running `day36_38_bias_testing.py` on 2026-08-08 (same seed, same 80-image sample) found every quality-pass-rate number had moved upward since 2026-08-04, while passive-liveness numbers stayed bit-identical: Quality pass rate Female 32.5% (13/40) vs. Male 55.0% (22/40); skin tone Dark 38.5% (10/26) vs. Light 50.0% (14/28); passive liveness Senior 25.0% (6/24) vs. Middle-aged 46.2% (12/26), unchanged. Consistent with (not confirmed as caused by) commit `f1b7007`'s quality-scoring changes landing in that window — `src/liveness_passive.py` did not change. **The gap direction and rough size are unchanged** (Male still ~22.5 points above Female; Dark still ~11.5 points below Light) — still unmitigated, just with current numbers on record instead of stale ones.
* **Update (2026-08-08, Phase 4 — root-caused per-sub-score, not just restated):** extended `day36_38_bias_testing.py` to break down `compute_quality_score()`'s 7 sub-scores (brightness/blur/pose/position/occlusion/contrast/resolution) by subgroup, plus `check_brightness()`'s raw grayscale-intensity measurement specifically (darker skin reflecting less light back to a camera is a well-documented source of exactly this kind of measurement bias, and cheap to check against data already collected). Full output: `scratch/bias_subscore_breakdown_output.txt`.
  * **Skin-tone gap (Dark 38.5% vs. Light 50.0% quality pass rate): brightness is the single largest weighted driver.** Weighted per-check contribution to the Light-minus-Dark score gap (raw sub-score gap × that check's weight in `compute_quality_score()`): brightness +1.74 of a ~4.36 total (~40%), pose +0.92, contrast +0.74, resolution +0.57, occlusion +0.54, blur −0.15 (not a driver, slightly favors Dark). The raw measurement confirms the mechanism directly: mean grayscale intensity was 110.4 for Dark-skin images vs. 120.1 (Light) / 124.8 (Medium) — Dark sits closer to the system's `BRIGHTNESS_MIN=100` floor, consistent with cameras under-exposing darker skin tones on average.
  * **Gender gap (Female 32.5% vs. Male 55.0% quality pass rate): a completely different driver — blur/sharpness, not brightness.** Weighted contribution to the Male-minus-Female gap: blur +5.60 of a ~6.19 total (~90%) — Male images measured a mean blur/sharpness sub-score of 35.6 vs. Female's 13.2. Brightness actually runs the *opposite* direction here (−1.32, i.e. slightly favors Female) — proof the two subgroup gaps are not the same phenomenon and don't share a cause. Why the CFP dataset's male vs. female images differ in measured sharpness has not been investigated further (out of this phase's scope) — reported as an open question, not attributed to a cause that hasn't been verified.
  * **Age gap (Senior 25.0% vs. Middle-aged 46.2%) lives in passive liveness, not quality** — Senior's quality pass rate (45.8%) is nearly identical to Middle-aged's (46.2%), so this sub-score breakdown (which only covers `compute_quality_score()`) has no explanatory power for the age gap by construction; its cause would need to be sought inside MiniFASNet's own behavior, not the quality checks.
  * **Client-facing disclosure paragraph (plain English, grounded in the specific driving checks, drafted this pass):**
    > "Two separate accuracy gaps were measured across demographic groups in this system's face-quality and liveness checks, and they have different, specific causes — not one general 'the system is biased' issue. The skin-tone gap (darker-skin images passing the quality check less often) is driven mainly by the brightness measurement: images of people with darker skin measured lower average brightness in our test sample, sitting closer to the system's minimum-brightness cutoff. This is a well-documented pattern in cameras generally — darker skin reflects less light back to the sensor, so the same fixed brightness threshold affects it more often, even though the measurement itself treats every image identically. The gender gap (fewer images passing for the women in our sample) has a different cause entirely: it's driven by the sharpness measurement, not brightness, for reasons we have not yet identified. Neither gap has been corrected — no per-group threshold adjustment or retraining has been done — this is disclosed as an open, unmitigated finding, not treated as resolved."
  * **Status: still UNMITIGATED, as originally decided** — this pass adds root cause and current numbers, not a fix. Full rebalancing/retraining remains explicitly out of scope per the original 2026-08-04 decision.

### Finding: Intermittent WebRTC Connection Hiccup (environmental, not an app bug)
* Reproduced 3 times across separate sessions using a targeted probe on `ctx.state.playing` plus a frame-arrival heartbeat in `video_frame_callback`. Every time: `ctx.state.playing` flips `True` -> `False` for 20-32 seconds, then self-recovers, with frame arrival stopping and resuming in exact lockstep (e.g., last frame at t=514.79s, next at t=546.74s, a 32s gap matching the `playing=False` window precisely).
* The app's own polling loop (`if ctx.state.playing and keep_polling_alive: ... st.rerun()`) was confirmed working exactly as designed — it correctly pauses reruns during the drop and resumes after, so this is not a script freeze or polling-loop bug.
* Ruled out: no exception/traceback or ICE/connection-state log line during the stall window (aiortc/streamlit-webrtc don't surface this transition at the captured log level); zero Windows Defender or System event log entries in the exact wall-clock window; `aiortc` 1.15.0 / `streamlit-webrtc` 0.76.2 are both current, not a known-buggy stale version.
* **Conclusion**: most likely an aiortc peer-connection ICE consent-freshness/keepalive hiccup, self-healing without app intervention. Observed in every longer session tested, typically 30-90s into a connection, lasting 20-32s. There is no code-level cause identified to fix — this is a library/environment-level characteristic, not an application bug.
* **Status: KNOWN LIMITATION, not chased further** (diminishing-return risk this close to delivery outweighs an unconfirmed speculative fix). **Practical mitigation for recording**: keep continuous camera segments under ~30 seconds where possible, or be ready for one hard cut/retry if it hits mid-take. Disclose to the client as a known WebRTC characteristic rather than presenting the app as glitch-free.
* **Update (2026-08-07, post-dates this entry):** a targeted mitigation was attempted in `app/streamlit_app.py` (~line 40) — `aioice.ice.CONSENT_FAILURES` raised from its default of 6 to 20, since `CONSENT_INTERVAL(5s) * CONSENT_FAILURES(6) ≈ 30s` lines up almost exactly with the observed hiccup window found by this same investigation. Same root-cause finding as above, not a second/different one — the current report's "what's still open" description of this item (a "targeted tolerance increase applied as a testable mitigation — not a confirmed fix") refers to this exact change, so this worksheet entry and the report are now one source of truth rather than two slightly different descriptions. Effectiveness has not yet been re-measured with a fresh before/after probe (that re-measurement is Phase 2 scope, not done here).
* **Update (2026-08-08, Phase 2 — app-notices-recovery gap found and fixed; mitigation re-measurement inconclusive):**
  * **App-side bug found and fixed, independent of the mitigation's effectiveness.** Read through `streamlit_webrtc`'s component source (`venv/Lib/site-packages/streamlit_webrtc/component.py`): the frontend does push an independent value-change + rerun when its `playing` state changes, via the standard Streamlit component protocol (`_make_state_change_callback`) — but `_handle_worker_lifecycle`'s "Stop" branch tears the worker down (`_reset_context()`) the moment `not context.state.playing and not context.state.signalling`, and this app's own polling loop (`if ctx.state.playing and keep_polling_alive: ... _safe_polling_rerun()`) also stops scheduling further reruns the moment `ctx.state.playing` goes `False`. Net effect, confirmed empirically via three live Playwright soaks (180-300s each, `DEBUG_WEBRTC`-logged every tick + a frame-arrival heartbeat, same method as the original 2026-08-04 investigation): **once the camera drops, nothing in this app causes the fragment to run again on its own.** In every soak, logging simply stopped dead at the drop and never resumed for the remainder of the (up to 300s) observation window, even though the Streamlit server itself stayed fully responsive throughout (confirmed via a plain HTTP GET). This is a real gap, not just cosmetic — even in a world where the underlying connection genuinely self-heals, the UI would sit frozen on stale state until the user clicked something. **Fixed**: `app/streamlit_app.py`'s "ACTIVE RERUN TRIGGER LOOP" now has a second branch — when `cam_ever_started` is true and `ctx.state.playing` is false, it reschedules on a slower 0.8s cadence (vs the normal 0.08s) instead of going silent. CPU cost measured directly (`Get-Process -Id <streamlit_pid> | Select CPU`, 5s samples): ~2.8 CPU-seconds/5s while actively polling vs ~0.05 CPU-seconds/5s on the new idle-reschedule branch — about 60x lower, not "noticeably more."
  * **Also added**: a distinguishable "Reconnecting... this can briefly take up to 30 seconds" message (was previously the same "click Start" text as a genuine never-started camera — actively misleading during a self-recovering drop), and a clean reset of `active_challenge_*`/`rppg_*` session state the moment a drop is detected mid-attempt, so a real challenge/rPPG window in progress fails with a friendly "Connection hiccup — let's try that again" message instead of silently blowing through `ACTIVE_CHALLENGE_TIMEOUT_S`/`RPPG_TIMEOUT_S` once ticks resume and reading as a genuine (false) liveness failure.
  * **Mitigation re-measurement — inconclusive, reported honestly rather than claimed as validated or refuted.** All three synthetic soaks (Playwright headless Chromium + `--use-file-for-fake-video-capture`, same harness used throughout this project) showed a drop, but the pattern looked different from the original 2026-08-04 finding in two ways: drops landed **much earlier** (≈10-19s after connecting, vs. the documented "typically 30-90s"), and **none recovered within the observation window** (up to ~300s watched, vs. the documented 20-32s self-recovery). A `True → False` transition this early doesn't line up with `CONSENT_INTERVAL(5s) × CONSENT_FAILURES(20) ≈ 80-120s` — if the drop were still caused by the same aioice consent-check mechanism the mitigation targets, it shouldn't fire this fast. The more likely explanation is that this synthetic environment (headless Chromium + a looping static-file fake camera device, run repeatedly in quick succession) is triggering a **different, harder instability specific to the test harness** — not a reproduction of the original real-camera/real-browser phenomenon. **This is not evidence the mitigation failed, and it is not evidence it worked** — it's evidence that this test method cannot cleanly exercise the same code path the original investigation found. A real validation requires the original method: a human on a real camera/browser, timing genuine sessions, the same way the 2026-08-04 finding was originally produced. That has not been done as of this update.
  * **Status unchanged**: still a known, disclosed environmental characteristic, not a confirmed-fixed item. What changed this pass is that the app now behaves correctly *around* the hiccup (notices recovery promptly if it happens, doesn't mislead the user, doesn't false-fail an in-progress attempt) — independent of whether the underlying `CONSENT_FAILURES` tuning itself is doing anything.

### Finding: Screen-Replay Liveness Gap — full-pipeline measured, a major evaluation-methodology bug found along the way, a new supplementary signal added (2026-08-08, Phase 3)

* **A live-pipeline test found the compounding scenario the report already flagged as a risk is real.** Built 10 video attack variants from the project's one real screen-replay capture (`screen_001.jpg`) — sharpened/brightened to plausibly clear the quality gate, some with a synthetic blink spliced over the real detected eye landmarks — and drove each through the actual Verify Identity flow via Playwright + a fake camera device (`scratch/drive_screen_replay_attacks.py`), not a batch script. 0/10 fully bypassed in that run, but a direct, controlled test of `evaluate_blink_tick()` confirmed the spliced blink **does** register a pass under favorable tick timing — the live 0/10 result reflects a low per-attempt probability (a ~0.5-1s tick landing inside a ~0.6s blink window), not a robust defense. None of the 10 attempts reached rPPG, so rPPG's effectiveness against this specific compound attack is still untested.

* **Major finding along the way, bigger than the screen-replay question itself: `data/Evaluation_Report.md` Section 5.1's APCER/BPCER numbers do not describe what the live app actually does.** Section 5.1 computes attack-detection rates by thresholding `antispoof_score` at 0.90. The live app's real decision (`src/liveness_passive.py`'s `check_passive_liveness()`, used by `src/pipeline.py` and the app) uses DeepFace's own `is_real` boolean directly and never reads or thresholds `antispoof_score` anywhere in application code. Confirmed with concrete counter-examples that rule out a simple threshold relationship: `screen_001.jpg` scores 0.9712 yet `is_real=False` (correctly rejected); `video_001.jpg` scores only 0.5262 yet `is_real=True` (incorrectly accepted) — score does not predict the real decision's direction. Re-measured against the real signal (same 76-attack/75-genuine images, same RNG seed, `scratch/run_expanded_eval_with_screen_check.py`): screen-replay-laptop is actually missed only 5.3% of the time (much better than the reported 73.7%), but screen-replay-phone — previously reported as reliably caught at 5.3% — is missed 63.2% of the time by the real decision. Full detail and the corrected numbers are in `data/Evaluation_Report.md` Section 5.1.3 (the correction) and 5.1.4 (the new signal below); this worksheet entry does not repeat the full tables. **Not fixed here**: reconciling Section 5.1.1/5.1.2's threshold-sweep methodology against the real `is_real` signal is flagged as its own follow-up, out of scope for this pass.

* **New supplementary signal added**: `check_screen_surface_texture()` (`src/quality_checks.py`) — cheap, non-ML, measures spatial uniformity of local sharpness (a display's pixel grid imposes a more uniform sharpness ceiling than real skin's naturally uneven micro-texture). Calibrated against real measurements (genuine: 1.099-1.183; real screen-replay-derived: 0.567-0.846; `TEXTURE_UNIFORMITY_MIN = 0.90` sits in the gap — full calibration comment in the source). Wired into `src/pipeline.py`'s `run_liveness_stage()` (Verify Identity's real path) and `app/streamlit_app.py`'s `verify_pose_and_quality()` (Guided Enrollment's path) as an **additional** signal — MiniFASNet's own `is_real` result is untouched, either check failing rejects. Measured effect: closes the screen-replay-phone gap from 63.2% missed to 0% missed in the same 76-sample set, with zero new false rejections across 75 genuine samples. Does not change the already-well-handled laptop category or the unrelated printed-photo/frozen-frame categories (expected — it's a screen-specific signal).

* **Sample-size caveat, same discipline as every other real-data calibration in this project**: only two independent real screen-replay base captures exist (`screen_001.jpg` laptop, `video_001.jpg` phone) — the new threshold has not been tested against a genuinely different physical screen, room, or distance. `docs/screen_replay_capture_checklist.md` specifies what a real capture session needs to validate this further (different displays, lighting, distances, angles, both photo and video content) — **not executed, needs a human with a camera, explicitly not attempted or fabricated here.**

* **Status: screen-replay-phone gap substantially mitigated and measured; screen-replay-laptop was less of a problem than previously reported; active-liveness blink-timing compounding confirmed real but not fixed (that's report item #2's existing scope, not this item's); rPPG's effectiveness against this specific compound attack remains unmeasured; the broader APCER methodology correction is a flagged follow-up, not resolved.**
